🧪 Systematic Cation Identification & Separation

Deduce cations by adding aqueous NaOH followed by aqueous NH₃ dropwise until in excess.

Cu²⁺ (Copper II)
• NaOH: Pale blue precipitate, insoluble in excess.
• NH₃(aq): Pale blue precipitate, soluble in excess to form a deep royal blue solution [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺.
Fe²⁺ (Iron II)
• NaOH: Dirty green precipitate, insoluble in excess.
• NH₃(aq): Dirty green precipitate, insoluble in excess (turns brown on standing in air).
Fe³⁺ (Iron III)
• NaOH: Red-brown precipitate, insoluble in excess.
• NH₃(aq): Red-brown precipitate, insoluble in excess.
Zn²⁺ (Zinc)
• NaOH: White precipitate, soluble in excess [Zn(OH)₄]²⁻.
• NH₃(aq): White precipitate, soluble in excess [Zn(NH₃)₄]²⁺.
Al³⁺ & Pb²⁺
• NaOH: White precipitate, soluble in excess.
• NH₃(aq): White precipitate, insoluble in excess.
• Distinguish Pb²⁺: Add KI(aq) → Golden yellow ppt (PbI₂).
Ca²⁺ & Mg²⁺
• NaOH: White precipitate, insoluble in excess.
• NH₃(aq): Ca²⁺ gives NO precipitate. Mg²⁺ gives slight white precipitate.

🔥 KNEC Flame Emission Spectroscopy & Cation Confirmatory Tests

KNEC Paper 3 Qualitative Standard

Excitation of metal ions in a non-luminous Bunsen burner flame produces characteristic emission spectra due to electrons returning from excited energy levels to ground states.

FIG 2.1 — FLAME TEST APPARATUS SCHEMATIC
📋 KICD Three-Step Flame Test Procedure
  1. Clean Glass Rod: Dip a clean glass rod into concentrated HCl and hold the tip in a non-luminous Bunsen flame until no colour is imparted.
  2. Pick Salt Sample: Moisten the tip of the cleaned glass rod in conc. HCl and touch a small crystal of the unknown solid salt.
  3. Observe Flame Emission: Introduce the glass rod tip into the hottest outer zone of the non-luminous flame and observe the persistent characteristic flame colour produced.
KICD Syllabus Standard: A clean glass rod is the standard laboratory instrument used in Kenyan schools to test metal cations in a non-luminous flame with concentrated HCl.

📐 Physical Chemistry Formulae & Volumetric Concordancy Rubric

Essential mathematical relationships and examiner marking standards for KCSE Paper 3.

⚖️ Titration Concordancy Standard
• Concordancy Criterion: Titres must agree within ±0.10 cm³ (acceptable ±0.20 cm³).
• Average Titre Calculation: Titreavg = (V₁ + V₂) / 2 using strictly concordant values.
• Molarity Equation: (Ma · Va) / na = (Mb · Vb) / nb.
🔥 Thermochemistry Enthalpy (ΔH)
• Heat Absorbed/Evolved: Q = m · c · ΔT (c = 4.2 J/g·°C, ρ = 1.0 g/cm³).
• Molar Enthalpy: ΔH = -(Q / (n × 1000)) kJ/mol.
• Sign Convention: Negative (–ΔH) = Exothermic; Positive (+ΔH) = Endothermic.
⚡ Reaction Rates & Solubility Curves
• Reaction Rate: Rate = 1 / time (s⁻¹).
• Solubility Definition: Max grams of solute dissolved in 100g of H₂O at temperature T.
• Crystallization Temperature: First appearance of crystals on gradual cooling.
