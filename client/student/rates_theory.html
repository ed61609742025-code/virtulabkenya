Rates of Reaction & Reversible Reactions

KNEC KCSE Chemistry Form 4 Topic 1 • Complete Revision Guide & Marking Schemes

⚛️ 1. Collision Theory & Activation Energy

Chemical reactions occur when reactant particles collide. However, not all collisions result in a chemical reaction. For a collision to be effective (successful), two fundamental conditions must be met:

KNEC KCSE Definition — Effective Collision Criteria:
  1. Sufficient Energy: Colliding particles must possess energy equal to or greater than the Activation Energy (\(E_a\)).
  2. Correct Steric Orientation: Colliding particles must collide with the proper geometry/alignment to allow bond breaking and bond formation.
Rate of Reaction = (Frequency of Collisions) × (Fraction of Effective Collisions)

Activation Energy (\(E_a\))

The minimum amount of energy required by colliding reactant particles to break existing bonds and form the unstable, high-energy activated complex.

📈 2. Maxwell-Boltzmann Energy Distribution

At any given temperature, molecules in a sample do not all have the same kinetic energy. Their kinetic energies are distributed according to the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution curve:

Effect of Temperature on the Curve (KCSE Paper 2 Common Question):
  • When temperature is increased from \(T_1\) to \(T_2\):
  • The peak of the curve shifts to the right and is lower/flatter.
  • The total area under the curve remains constant (same total number of particles).
  • A significantly larger fraction of molecules have kinetic energy \(\ge E_a\) (area to the right of \(E_a\) increases).
  • Conclusion: Frequency of effective collisions increases dramatically, leading to an exponential increase in the rate of reaction.

🔬 3. Five Factors Affecting Reaction Rates

1. Concentration / Pressure

Increasing concentration of solutions (or pressure of gases) increases the number of particles per unit volume \(\rightarrow\) particles are closer together \(\rightarrow\) collision frequency per unit time increases \(\rightarrow\) rate increases.

2. Temperature

Increases average kinetic energy and velocity of particles \(\rightarrow\) larger proportion of particles exceed \(E_a\) \(\rightarrow\) frequency of effective collisions increases.

3. Surface Area (Particle Size)

Fine powders have a much greater total surface area exposed to acid compared to large marble chips \(\rightarrow\) more contact points for collisions \(\rightarrow\) higher initial reaction rate. (Note: Total gas yield remains identical if reactant masses are equal).

4. Catalysts

A substance that increases the rate of a chemical reaction by providing an alternative pathway with a lower activation energy (\(E_a\)), without itself being consumed. Biological catalysts are called enzymes (e.g. catalase), which denature at high temperatures (\(>45^\circ\text{C}\)).

5. Light Intensity (Photochemical Reactions)

Light photons provide the necessary activation energy to dissociate bonds (e.g. silver halide photographic darkening, reaction of methane with chlorine in UV light).

⚖️ 4. Reversible Reactions & Le Chatelier's Principle

A reversible reaction is one in which the products can react to reform the original reactants: \(\text{A} + \text{B} \rightleftharpoons \text{C} + \text{D}\).

Le Chatelier's Principle: When a system in dynamic chemical equilibrium is subjected to a change in condition (concentration, temperature, pressure), the system shifts in such a direction as to oppose or neutralize the effect of that change.

Industrial Applications in KCSE:

  1. The Haber Process (Ammonia): \(\text{N}_2(g) + 3\text{H}_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_3(g) \quad (\Delta H = -92\,\text{kJ/mol})\)
    Optimum Conditions: \(450^\circ\text{C}\), \(200\,\text{atm}\), Finely divided Iron (\(\text{Fe}\)) catalyst.
  2. The Contact Process (Sulfur Trioxide): \(2\text{SO}_2(g) + \text{O}_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2\text{SO}_3(g) \quad (\Delta H = -197\,\text{kJ/mol})\)
    Optimum Conditions: \(450^\circ\text{C}\), \(1-2\,\text{atm}\), Vanadium(V) oxide (\(\text{V}_2\text{O}_5\)) catalyst.

📐 5. KNEC Paper 3 Graphing Secrets (SAPC Rubric)

Examiner Scoring Breakdown (4.0 Marks Total):
  • Scale (S) — 1.0m: Must occupy at least half of the grid in both dimensions. Use standard intervals (1:1, 1:2, 1:5, 1:10). Avoid awkward scales like 1:3 or 1:7!
  • Axes (A) — 0.5m: Must have correct labels with physical units on both axes (e.g. \(1/t \, (\text{s}^{-1})\) on y-axis and \(\text{Volume} \, (\text{cm}^3)\) on x-axis).
  • Plotting (P) — 1.0m: Plot points with neat small crosses ('x') within \(\pm \frac{1}{2}\) small square.
  • Curve/Line (C) — 1.5m: For \(1/t\) vs. Volume, draw a straight line passing through the origin \((0,0)\). For Volume vs. Time, draw a smooth continuous curve (never use a ruler to connect curved points).
Instantaneous Rate Tangent Calculation:

To find the rate at \(t = 60\,\text{s}\):

  1. Draw a straight tangent touching the curve only at \(t = 60\,\text{s}\).
  2. Construct a large right-angled triangle on the tangent line.
  3. Show dashed projection lines to the axes.
  4. Calculate slope: \(\text{Rate} = \frac{\Delta V}{\Delta t} = \frac{V_2 - V_1}{t_2 - t_1} \, \text{cm}^3/\text{s}\).
