KNEC 233/3 Senior Examiner Guidelines & Dos/Don'ts

Strict Penalties (Marks Deducted)
  • "White Solution": Never write "white solution" (Deduction: -0.5 Mk). Solutions without precipitate are colorless, precipitates are white.
  • Missing Ionic Charges: Element symbols written without ionic charges (e.g. Pb instead of Pb²⁺, SO4 instead of SO₄²⁻) forfeit the inference mark (-0.5 Mk).
  • Contradictory Ions: Claiming colored ions (Cu²⁺, Fe²⁺, Fe³⁺) when a white precipitate was observed, or claiming Zn²⁺ when a precipitate is insoluble in aqueous NH₃, incurs an automatic -0.5 to -1.0 mark penalty.
  • Vague Gas Observations: "Gas is evolved" without specifying effervescence, odor, color, or reaction with moist litmus / limewater / acidified dichromate will not score.
Mandatory Examination Phrases
  • Always Specify Excess Reagent: For NaOH(aq) and NH₃(aq), you must write both the initial precipitate AND its behavior in excess (e.g., "White precipitate soluble in excess to form a colorless solution").
  • Distinguish Clear vs Colorless: A solution can be clear blue; if it looks like water, write "colorless solution".
  • Gas Evolution: Write "Effervescence of a colorless, odorless gas that turns calcium hydroxide (limewater) milky" for CO₂.
  • Amphoteric Cations: When a white ppt dissolves in excess NaOH, infer Zn²⁺, Al³⁺, Pb²⁺. Differentiate them using aqueous NH₃ and KI.
