Cation Tests & Separation

Deduce cations by adding aqueous NaOH followed by aqueous NH₃ dropwise until in excess.

Cu²⁺ (Copper II)
• NaOH: Pale blue precipitate, insoluble in excess.
• NH₃(aq): Pale blue precipitate, soluble in excess to form a deep royal blue solution [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺.
Fe²⁺ (Iron II)
• NaOH: Dirty green precipitate, insoluble in excess.
• NH₃(aq): Dirty green precipitate, insoluble in excess (turns brown in air).
Fe³⁺ (Iron III)
• NaOH: Red-brown precipitate, insoluble in excess.
• NH₃(aq): Red-brown precipitate, insoluble in excess.
Zn²⁺ (Zinc)
• NaOH: White precipitate, soluble in excess [Zn(OH)₄]²⁻.
• NH₃(aq): White precipitate, soluble in excess [Zn(NH₃)₄]²⁺.
Al³⁺ & Pb²⁺
• NaOH: White precipitate, soluble in excess.
• NH₃(aq): White precipitate, insoluble in excess.
• Distinguish Pb²⁺: Add KI(aq) → Yellow ppt (PbI₂).
Ca²⁺ & Mg²⁺
• NaOH: White precipitate, insoluble in excess.
• NH₃(aq): Ca²⁺ gives no precipitate. Mg²⁺ gives slight white precipitate.

Flame Emission Tests

Qualitative Standard

Excitation of metal ions in a non-luminous Bunsen burner flame produces characteristic emission colours.

FLAME TEST APPARATUS SCHEMATIC
Three-Step Flame Test Procedure
  1. Clean Glass Rod: Dip a clean glass rod into concentrated HCl and hold the tip in a non-luminous Bunsen flame until no colour is imparted.
  2. Dip into Salt Solution: Dip the clean glass rod into the aqueous solution of the unknown salt to pick up a liquid droplet.
  3. Observe Flame Emission: Place the glass rod tip with the solution droplet into the non-luminous Bunsen flame and observe the persistent characteristic flame colour produced.
In the KNEC KCSE standard practical, a clean glass rod is dipped directly into the salt solution and introduced into a non-luminous Bunsen flame.
Sodium (Na⁺)
Persistent Golden Yellow
Masks other ions; intense emission.
Potassium (K⁺)
Lilac / Pale Violet

Calcium (Ca²⁺)

Distinct from crimson lithium.

Key Formulas & Volumetric Concordancy

Essential mathematical relationships for Paper 3 calculations.

Titration Concordancy Standard
• Concordancy Criterion: Titres must agree within ±0.10 cm³ (acceptable ±0.20 cm³).
• Average Titre Calculation: Titreavg = (V₁ + V₂) / 2 using concordant values.
• Molarity Equation: (Ma · Va) / na = (Mb · Vb) / nb.
Thermochemistry Enthalpy (ΔH)
• Heat Absorbed/Evolved: Q = m · c · ΔT (c = 4.2 J/g·°C, ρ = 1.0 g/cm³).
• Molar Enthalpy: ΔH = -(Q / (n × 1000)) kJ/mol.
• Sign Convention: Negative (–ΔH) = Exothermic; Positive (+ΔH) = Endothermic.
Reaction Rates & Solubility
• Reaction Rate: Rate = 1 / time (s⁻¹).
• Solubility Definition: Max grams of solute dissolved in 100g of H₂O at temperature T.
• Crystallization Temperature: First appearance of crystals on cooling.
